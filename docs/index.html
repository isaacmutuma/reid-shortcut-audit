Diagnosing and repairing shortcut learning
in person re-identification

A six-phase mechanistic audit of a ResNet50 re-ID baseline trained on Market-1501. Diagnoses clothing color as a primary shortcut, quantifies its severity, and repairs it — recovering full accuracy while nearly doubling robustness.

Finding
A linear probe achieves 92.9% clothing color accuracy on the final embedding — the same representation used for person matching. Color is a primary, linearly separable feature throughout the network, consistent with shortcut learning.
Phase 4 — What happens when clothing changes color
Baseline Repaired
Baseline drops from 65.3% to 23.1% at 90 degrees. Repaired model stays at 44.7%.
Finding — the cliff vs the slope
The baseline collapses from 65.3% to 23.1% mAP at 90° hue shift — a 42 point drop. The repaired model degrades from 65.5% to 44.7% — a flatter curve. In deployment, graceful degradation is the difference between a usable system and a broken one.

Key insight — you cannot fine-tune away a deep shortcut
Fine-tuning from a shortcut-heavy checkpoint with mild augmentation (±30°) failed entirely — the shortcut was too deeply encoded to overwrite. Training from scratch with strong color augmentation (±90°) matched baseline accuracy while nearly doubling robustness. The shortcut must be prevented, not corrected.
